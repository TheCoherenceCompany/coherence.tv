	Market Segment Profile: Organisational Transformation, Culture Activation and Collective Intelligence
1. TL;DR
This segment is companies undergoing change, and the consultants or HR transformation partners who help them, where leaders need to hear more of the organisation’s real intelligence rather than relying only on executives, surveys, or formal workshops. The Coherence Company can serve them through guided “coherence conversations” that help surface knowledge, trust, tensions, opportunities, culture patterns, product insight, go-to-market ideas, and AI adoption needs. The key challenge is trust: the organisations that need this most may also be the least ready for open conversation. That makes trusted facilitators, pilots, and carefully designed use cases essential.
2. Market Segment Overview
The segment has two related customer groups.
The first is companies and organisations directly. These may include businesses, hospitals and or public sector organizations, Municipalities, large teams, scaleups, professional organisations, or institutions trying to improve culture, adopt AI, launch new initiatives, understand internal problems, or involve more people in strategic sensemaking.
The second is service providers: culture transformation firms, HR consultants, organisational development specialists, leadership facilitators, AI adoption advisors, and boutique consultancies like the FLOW Group. These actors may become the stronger early route to market because they already hold trust with clients and can introduce coherence conversations as part of a wider transformation process.
This segment is distinct from generic enterprise software because the product is not just a tool. It touches trust, voice, behaviour, internal politics, culture, and organisational learning. It is also distinct from ordinary surveys or 360 feedback tools because it is more conversational, contextual, and sensemaking-oriented.
Assumption: The best-fit organisations are likely mid-sized to large enough that leaders no longer have direct access to everyone’s reality, but not so bureaucratic that experimentation is impossible.
3. Core Problems and Pain Points
Surface-level problems
Companies struggle with:
* Low-quality internal feedback.
* Poor cross-functional understanding.
* Weak employee engagement in transformation programmes.
* AI adoption efforts that ignore culture and human readiness.
* Product and go-to-market decisions made by too few people.
* Traditional 360s that feel mechanical or shallow.
* Leadership teams not knowing what people really think.
* Transformation workshops that do not reach enough of the organisation.
* Internal knowledge trapped in people’s heads, teams, and informal networks.
Deeper underlying tensions
The deeper issue is that organisations often need collective intelligence but do not have the trust conditions to access it.
Antonio’s strongest insight is that the companies most in need of coherence conversations may be the least able to use them at first. In toxic, fearful, or low-agency environments, people will not speak openly just because a tool asks them to. They will say what is politically safe.
The transcript also contains a powerful culture insight: culture is not an abstract value system; it is made of observable behaviours. People keep behaviours that help them survive. If a company asks people to behave differently without making that change feel safe, they will resist.
So the real problem is not “we need better conversations.” It is:
How do we create enough trust for people to reveal useful truth, and then turn that truth into better organisational action?
4. Values and Motivations
This segment cares about practical improvement, but through human systems.
Business values
They care about better decisions, stronger transformation outcomes, faster learning, healthier culture, more effective AI adoption, better product direction, and stronger go-to-market alignment. They care about results; but how much is their culture fostering or preventing them.
Cultural values
The healthier buyers care about trust, openness, psychological safety, autonomy, responsible participation, and real listening. They do not only want people to comply with change; they want people to engage with it.
Decision-making values
They will need credibility, safety, clear facilitation, strong privacy boundaries, and proof that the process produces usable insight rather than vague sentiment.
What they are trying to protect or improve
They are trying to protect the organisation from failed transformation, internal cynicism, wasted knowledge, cultural toxicity, and leadership blindness.
Future they are moving toward
They are moving toward organisations where more people can contribute intelligence to strategy, culture, product, AI adoption, and change, ultimately to results — without every decision needing to come from “the usual bosses.”
5. Desired Outcomes
Success looks like an organisation that can listen to itself more honestly and act on what it learns.
Desired outcomes include:
* Leaders understand what people actually experience; and namely under their actions.
* Employees feel more seen and less treated as passive recipients of change.
* Transformation efforts meet less hidden resistance.
* Culture work becomes tied to behaviours, not slogans.
* AI adoption, innovation, change, includes human readiness and organisational context.
* Product, marketing, and go-to-market teams benefit from wider internal intelligence.
* Consultants get a richer diagnostic and engagement tool.
* 360 feedback becomes more human, contextual, and developmental.
* The organisation moves from controlled communication to safer, structured sensemaking.
The deeper transformation is:
From organisation as hierarchy of decisions/formal power to organisation as a living intelligence system.
6. Benefits of the Startup’s Offering
Functional benefits
The Coherence Company can provide guided conversation formats that help companies gather insight from many people across the organisation. These conversations can support culture diagnostics, AI readiness, product input, go-to-market learning, transformation processes, feedback, and next-generation 360 processes.
The value is not simply recording conversations. It is turning many human inputs into patterns, themes, summaries, signals, and possible actions.
Strategic benefits
The offering helps companies access intelligence that is normally invisible to leadership. This can improve strategy, transformation design and implementation, product development, internal communication, and change sequencing.
For consultants, it becomes a tool in the transformation toolbox: a way to deepen diagnosis, extend engagement before or after in-person workshops, and support more grounded interventions.
Financial or growth benefits
For companies, the financial value may come through better transformation ROI, reduced change failure, clearer paths to follow, enhanced employee engagement, stronger AI adoption, better product decisions, and improved retention or engagement.
For consultancies, the offering can create new billable modules, deepen client relationships, differentiate their methodology, and increase the value of transformation programmes.
Cultural or human benefits
The strongest human benefit is that people may feel heard in a way that is much more meaningful and far more impactful than a survey.
When done well, coherence conversations can help people move from defensiveness to participation. They can also help surface what people are afraid to say in standard formats — but only if trust and safety are properly designed.
Long-term ecosystem benefits
Over time, this could create a new organisational capability: companies that regularly use structured human-AI sensemaking to understand themselves, adapt, and coordinate better.
This aligns with The Coherence Company’s broader thesis: AI should help people work together more intelligently, not merely automate tasks.
7. Buying Triggers
This segment becomes ready to buy when an organisation or consultant faces a live transformation need.
Likely buying triggers include:
* A culture change programme is beginning.
* A company is adopting AI and wants to understand internal readiness.
* Leadership senses disengagement, fear, or cynicism.
* A transformation programme is meeting resistance.
* A merger, restructuring, or strategic shift creates uncertainty.
* A company wants more participatory product or go-to-market insight.
* A consultancy wants a stronger diagnostic and engagement process.
* A client has already built some trust with an external facilitator.
* Traditional surveys or 360s feel insufficient.
* Leaders say: “We need to know what people are really thinking.”
The sharpest trigger is:
“We need the organisation’s intelligence, but we do not trust our current methods to surface it.”
8. Objections and Risks
The biggest objection will be trust.
People may ask:
* Will employees speak honestly?
* Will this feel safe?
* Will leadership misuse the results?
* Is this just another HR listening exercise?
* Will it create more tension than we can handle?
* Will people talk to a machine?
* Is the process too complex to run?
* How do we protect confidentiality?
* How do we turn conversations into action?
* What is the ROI compared with surveys, workshops, or consulting interviews?
There is also a serious implementation risk: if coherence conversations are introduced too early in a low-trust environment, they may fail or even backfire. The transcript is clear that companies may first need a trusted facilitator to “win the crowd” before deeper conversation becomes possible.
Another risk is over-positioning. If sold as a magic culture fix, it will lose credibility. It should be positioned as a structured method for sensemaking, diagnosis, engagement, and organisational learning — not a replacement for leadership courage.
9. Best-Fit Customer Profile
The best-fit direct customer is:
A mid-sized or large organisation going through culture change, AI adoption, strategic transformation, product development, or go-to-market redesign, where leadership genuinely wants to hear from more people and is willing to act on what emerges.
The best-fit channel partner is:
A trusted culture transformation, HR, leadership, or organisational development consultancy that already has client trust and wants to add a powerful conversation-based sensemaking layer to its work.
The likely first buyers are not generic companies. They are brave internal champions or trusted external consultants who already understand that transformation depends on trust, behaviour, and participation.
10. Poor-Fit Customers
Poor-fit customers include:
* Organisations with highly toxic cultures and no leadership willingness to change.
* Companies that want the appearance of listening without real action.
* Leaders looking for surveillance disguised as engagement.
* HR teams that only want a cheaper survey tool.
* Organisations with no capacity to process or respond to what emerges.
* Companies that need strict confidentiality but lack the governance to protect it.
* Clients expecting AI to solve cultural problems without human facilitation.
* Teams that are too early in trust-building for open conversation.
The most dangerous poor-fit customer is one that says it wants openness but punishes honesty. The Coherence Company should avoid being used as theatre for false participation.
11. Messaging Angle
Main promise
Help your organisation hear itself clearly enough to change.
Alternative promise:
Turn internal conversations into trusted insight for culture, transformation, AI adoption, and better decisions.
Tone of voice
The tone should be grounded, serious, and trust-aware. This segment will not respond well to hype. The message should respect the difficulty of change.
Use phrases like:
* Organisational sensemaking.
* Culture diagnostics through conversation.
* Trusted transformation insight.
* From employee voice to actionable patterns.
* AI-supported listening, not AI surveillance.
* Surface the knowledge already inside your organisation.
* Help more people contribute to the future of the company.
* Extend transformation work beyond the workshop room.
* A richer alternative to surveys and static 360s.
Avoid:
* “AI will fix your culture.”
* “Radical transparency.”
* “Replace consultants.”
* “Fully automated transformation.”
* “Culture hacking.”
* “Employee intelligence mining.”
* Anything that sounds like monitoring, extraction, or forced vulnerability.
The message should make one thing clear: this is not about making people confess to a machine. It is about creating a well-designed process where human experience becomes useful organisational intelligence.
12. Segment Summary
This segment is attractive because companies are under pressure to change faster, adopt AI, improve culture, and make better use of internal knowledge. Yet most organisations still rely on thin tools: surveys, executive meetings, workshops, and consultant interviews. Coherence conversations could create a richer way to access the intelligence of the organisation.
Now is the right time because AI makes it possible to turn many conversations into patterns, summaries, insights, and next steps at a scale that was previously expensive or impractical. But the technology only works if paired with trust, facilitation, consent, and organisational maturity.
To win this market, The Coherence Company must prove three things:
1. People will participate honestly when the process is well designed.
2. The conversations produce insight that leaders, consultants, and teams can actually use.
3. The method improves transformation outcomes better than surveys, workshops, or standard 360 tools alone.
The cleanest strategic wedge is not “AI education for companies.” It is broader and stronger:
AI-supported coherence conversations for organisational transformation, culture, and collective intelligence. Authentic non-toxic working environment, necessarily better results.
The best early path may be through trusted transformation consultants who already know how to create safety, earn an audience, and help companies move from conversation to behavioural change.
